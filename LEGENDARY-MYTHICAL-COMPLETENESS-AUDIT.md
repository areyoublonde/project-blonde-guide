## Acquisition detail and ROM verification

### Ho-Oh

- **Starts:** On Route 39, near Moomoo Farm, a shadow passes overhead and Baoba asks what colour it was. Your answer picks your story legend. Gold is Ho-Oh.
- **Needs:** If you answered Gold: all eight Johto Badges, the Rainbow Wing and the Clear Bell. If you answered Silver: reach Pewter City in Kanto. The story gave you Lugia; Ho-Oh comes second.
- **Steps:**
  1. Gold. Clear the Goldenrod Radio Tower. The Director gives you the Rainbow Wing: see Goldenrod Radio Tower.
  2. With eight Badges, beat the Kimono Girls in Ecruteak’s theater. They give you the Clear Bell and go ahead to the tower: see The Kimono Girls.
  3. Go north through the gatehouse. The sage steps aside when he hears the bell. Cross Bellchime Trail and climb Tin Tower to the roof.
  4. Walk forward. The Kimono Girls dance and Ho-Oh descends. Talk to it from below.
  5. Silver. In Pewter City, talk to the old man. He gives you the Rainbow Wing he found in Johto. The Tin Tower’s sage now lets you through without a bell, and Ho-Oh is already on the roof at Level 70.
- **Encounter:** A single wild battle. It can be caught. Level 50 when it is your story legend, Level 70 from the Pewter old man’s wing and on every later return.
- **Afterwards:** If you catch or defeat it, it leaves and the Kimono Girls return to the theater. Ho-Oh is back on the roof at Level 70 after each later League win: How legendaries return.

### Raikou

- **Starts:** The basement of the Burned Tower in Ecruteak City.
- **Needs:** Reach Ecruteak City. No Badge is checked.
- **Steps:**
  1. Enter the Burned Tower and drop to the basement. Walk up to the three beasts: they wake and scatter. See Ecruteak City.
  2. From that moment Raikou is somewhere on Johto’s routes: Routes 29 to 39, 42 to 46 and 48. It is never in a town or a cave, and Routes 40, 41 and 47 are not on its path.
  3. Walk in the grass or Surf on those routes. When Raikou is on the route you are on, each wild encounter has a one-in-four chance of being Raikou instead.
  4. It changes route as you travel, and moves somewhere new after every battle.
- **Encounter:** A wild battle with its own music. It can be caught. Its HP and status are kept between meetings, so damage adds up. A Repel keeps it away if your leading Pokémon is above Level 40.
- **Afterwards:** Raikou is released again after each later League win, even if you already caught one: How legendaries return.

### Suicune

- **Starts:** Suicune does not roam. It shows itself in five places across two regions, then waits for you on Route 25.
- **Needs:** The Cascade Badge from Misty. Each sighting below, in order. They are on the story’s own path.
- **Steps:**
  1. Ecruteak City: wake the three beasts in the Burned Tower’s basement.
  2. Cianwood City: Suicune stands on the north shore. Walk up to it. It leaves, and Eusine challenges you: see Cianwood City.
  3. Route 42, between Ecruteak and Mahogany: it appears once more as you cross the route.
  4. Vermilion City, in Kanto: a sighting with Eusine as you walk through the city.

## Shared rules

### How legendaries return

Your first Hall of Fame entry, after the Johto League, does not reset anything. Every later one does. Once you hold sixteen Badges and re-enter the Indigo Plateau as described in The Kanto League, each Hall of Fame entry there puts the following back, whether you caught them before or not:
Back at their posts: Articuno, Zapdos, Moltres, Mewtwo, Ho-Oh and Lugia (both at Level 70), the Latias or Latios on Southern Island, Mew, Deoxys, the six Regis of Sinjoh including Regigigas, and the Mt. Moon rock that calls Jirachi.
Released again: Raikou and Entei in Johto and your chosen Latias or Latios in Kanto. A roamer that was still at large is replaced by a fresh one.
Only if you got far enough: Suicune returns to Route 25 once you have fought it there. A second Celebi appears in Ilex Forest once you have caught the first; if you did not catch it, the GS Ball reappears in the Ruins of Alph instead. The four Tapu return once Samson Oak has given you the Beast Ball. Arceus returns once Steven has congratulated you in its hall.
The same League win is also what makes you Kanto Champion the first time. That title is the key to three things on this page: the guard leaves Cerulean Cave, Steven hands over the Eon Ticket and a woman in Olivine City hands over the Aurora Ticket.
Kyogre, Groudon and Rayquaza follow Hoenn’s rule, not this one: see their entries.

### The seventeen plates of Sinjoh

1. Pick up the Azure Flute, an item ball on Mt. Silver 2F.
2. Go to the Pokémon Center outside Mt. Silver and talk to the researcher from the Pewter museum. She notices the flute, a window explains that this is extra content, and she asks whether you are ready. Say Yes. She takes you to a branch of the cave on 1F, where a scientist speaks to you and leaves. From now on she will take you there again whenever you ask.
3. Follow the branch into Snowswept Cavern. A Machamp stands by a boulder. Talk to it and accept: it fights at Level 75 and cannot be caught. When it goes down it drops the Fist Plate and the boulder is cleared. This is plate 1.
4. Carry on to Route 49, where a Kimono Girl meets you, then into the Kimono Girls’ hideout in New Sinjoh.
5. Go on to the research lab at the Sinjoh Ruins. Steven is there with a team from Devon. He explains that seventeen plates matter to the ruins and asks for your help. He keeps asking until you say Yes.
6. Seven Trainers now agree to battle, and each gives a plate when beaten: Skylar on Route 49 (Sky Plate); Miharu, Youko and Shizuko in the Kimono hideout (Meadow Plate, Icicle Plate, Pixie Plate); Mualani in the hot springs (Splash Plate); Wessel and Finley on Route 50 (Iron Plate, Draco Plate). Before you have agreed to help Steven they only talk.
7. Eight Noble Pokémon guard the rest. At each Noble’s spot the game says “The Azure Flute is reacting. Play the flute?” Say Yes and the Noble appears; talk to it to fight. All are Level 80, cannot be caught, and drop a plate: Overqwil (Dread Plate) and Wyrdeer (Mind Plate) on Route 49; Sneasler (Toxic Plate) in Snowswept Cavern; Hisuian Arcanine (Flame Plate) and Ursaluna (Earth Plate) in the hot springs; Kleavor (Insect Plate) and Hisuian Electrode (Zap Plate) on Route 50; Hisuian Typhlosion (Spooky Plate) in the old house in New Sinjoh.
8. Steven phones as the count rises. At 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15 plates a chamber under the ruins opens: Regirock, Regice, Registeel, Regieleki and Regidrago, in that order. You can go and catch each as it opens.
9. At 16 plates Steven calls you back. Enter the lab for a scene with the plates, after which you are taken outside to the ruins and a stairway opens.
10. Steven waits on the landing. Answer Yes when he asks if you are ready and beat him. He gives you the Stone Plate, the seventeenth, and heals your party. The hall above is now open: continue with Arceus.

## Battles that are not acquisitions

A scripted battle fought while the game's no-catching flag is set is not an acquisition source, and neither is an encounter script that Project Blonde has retired. Among Legendary and Mythical Pokémon the availability audit rejects these:

| Pokémon | Where | Why it is not a source |
|---|---|---|
| Rayquaza | Embedded Tower | source retired in Project Blonde |
| Kyogre | Route 19 Cave | source retired in Project Blonde |
| Groudon | Seafoam Islands Secret Cave | source retired in Project Blonde |
| Arceus (Steel form) | Sinjoh Ruins Arceus Room | scripted battle with catching disabled (FLAG_NO_WILD_CATCHING) |

The catch-disabled row is the first of Arceus's two battles: Level 100, Steel-type, no Ball and no escape. It unlocks the second battle (Level 80, Normal-type), which is the catchable one and is what the guide and the Pokédex record. The audit checks in the ROM that catching is switched back on before the second battle starts.

The retired rows are the old Heart & Soul sites for Kyogre, Groudon and Rayquaza, whose maps are no longer how those three are met. Their Hoenn encounters (Marine Cave, Terra Cave, Sky Pillar) are the sources the guide describes.

The Noble Pokémon and the Machamp of the Sinjoh story are also fought with catching disabled; none is Legendary. No Trainer in the game uses a Legendary or Mythical Pokémon.

During Hoenn's story Groudon, Kyogre and Rayquaza appear in scenes and leave without a battle. They become catchable only after the Hoenn League.

## Unresolved or uncertain

Nothing about *whether* a Pokémon is obtainable is uncertain. What remains unconfirmed is ground-level detail, because the natural playthrough caught only Ho-Oh:

- **Not walked.** Every entry marked *Scripts* or *Partly played* gives conditions read from the game's scripts. Routes inside dungeons (Whirl Islands, Seafoam Islands, Cerulean Cave, Mt. Silver's Moltres room, the Sky Pillar, Marine and Terra Cave) were not walked, so the guide gives the destination and a marked map rather than turn-by-turn directions.
- **Celebi's first step.** The GS Ball is released by the Ho-Oh panel puzzle in the Ruins of Alph. How early that room can be reached, and by which entrance, was not walked.
- **Sinjoh.** The story's stages, the seventeen plate sources and the chamber thresholds are read from the scripts. The exact tile of each Noble's flute prompt and the path between areas were not walked.
- **Mew's wharf.** The Old Sea Map's item ball is beside the truck at tile 61,46 of Vermilion City, on a wharf not joined to the land in the map image. That it is reached by Surf is read from the map, not played.
- **Night for Jirachi.** The rock tests the game's night flag. The clock hours that count as night are documented by the game's time system, not re-measured here.
- **Roamer odds.** One-in-four per wild encounter on the roamer's route, the Repel rule and the routes come from the engine source the ROM was built from; the route tables and levels are confirmed present in the ROM. The source also contains a rule that draws a roamer towards the player when Legendary Pokémon are in the party; that function could not be located in the ROM's symbol table, so the guide does not mention it.
- **Alternate formes.** See *Forms*.
- **A possible defect, not verified.** Arceus's first battle switches catching and running off and switches them back on only after the battle returns. If the player loses that battle and blacks out, the script does not reach the lines that switch them back on. Whether the engine restores them elsewhere was not tested. This is reported for the owner; the guide does not describe it.
